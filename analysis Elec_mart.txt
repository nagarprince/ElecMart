product & supplier :

Strong Analyst-Level Insights
1.High Supplier Dependency
The top 3 suppliers make up around 38% of total procurement spending (₹298M of ₹793M).
This means the company depends heavily on a few suppliers. If one has a problem, procurement could be affected.

2.Pending Purchase Orders
80% of POs are received, while 20% (30 POs) are still in transit or pending.
These pending orders should be monitored to avoid delays in inventory.

3.Supplier Cost vs. Delivery Time
Supplier delivery times range from around 7–10 days.
High-spending suppliers with longer delivery times need more attention because delays can affect inventory.

4.Overall Rating Hides Supplier Problems
The overall supplier rating is 4.42/5, which looks good.
However, some suppliers have higher defect rates, so each supplier should be checked separately.

5.Spending Concentrated on Few Suppliers
The top 6 suppliers contribute around 59% of total procurement spending.
The company could reduce risk by having more supplier options and negotiating better prices.

6.High Value per Purchase Order
Total procurement spending is ₹793M across 150 POs, which is about ₹5.29M per PO on average.
Because each PO has a high value, even a few delayed or problematic orders can have a big impact on the business.

Executive Dashboard – Key Analysis
1,Q4 was the strongest quarter
Revenue jumped from ₹59M in Q3 to ₹160M in Q4.
Orders and customers also increased a lot, so Q4 was clearly the best-performing quarter.

2.December needs attention
Q4 started very strongly, with revenue of around ₹72M in October and ₹67M in November.
But revenue fell to around ₹22M in December. I would check whether this was because of lower demand, stock issues, or any other business reason.

3.Profit increased along with sales
Profit went from around ₹10M in Q3 to ₹27M in Q4.
Even with the big increase in sales, the profit margin stayed around 16–17%, which is a positive sign.

4.Laptops and Smartphones are driving sales
These two categories generate much more revenue than the other categories.
They are the main products bringing revenue, but depending too much on them could also become a risk.

5.Customer base grew strongly in Q4
Customers increased from 476 in Q3 to around 1K in Q4.
This is a good sign, but the next focus should be on getting these new customers to purchase again.

6.Regular customers are the biggest group
Around 60% of customers are Regular customers.
Keeping these customers active and encouraging repeat purchases could help maintain future sales.

7.Returns stayed under control
The return rate stayed around 2–3% across the quarters.
Even though sales increased sharply in Q4, returns did not increase significantly, which is a good sign.

Inventory & Warehouse — Key Analyst Insights

1.Inventory is heavily concentrated
Mumbai and Ahmedabad together hold around ₹850M of inventory value, more than half of the total inventory.
A problem in either warehouse could have a major impact on the business.

2.Large storage capacity is going unused
Warehouse utilization is only 24.6%, meaning most storage capacity is still unused.
This suggests the company should improve inventory distribution before investing in more warehouse space.

3.Stockout risk is hidden inside total stock
The company has around 37K units in stock, but some products are already below their reorder levels.
So looking only at total stock can give a false sense of safety; product-level stock needs to be monitored.

4.Reserved and damaged stock reduce real availability
Around 3K units are reserved and 454 units are damaged.
This means a significant portion of total stock cannot be treated as freely available inventory.

5.High-value products need tighter stock planning
Smartphones and Laptops make up a large share of inventory value.
Keeping too much stock can block working capital, while keeping too little can cause lost sales—so these categories need better demand-based inventory planning.

1.Order Fulfillment & Logistics — Key Analyst Insights
Most orders are being delivered, but pending orders need attention
Around 2K orders are delivered, while 257 orders are still pending.
The business should track these pending orders closely to avoid further delays and customer complaints.

2.Delivery time is getting worse
Average delivery time increased from around 3.1 days in January to 3.6+ days in October–November.
This upward trend suggests that delivery efficiency may be getting weaker over time.

3.Returns are creating a major cost
The return rate is around 3.38%, but refunds have reached about ₹12M.
This means returns are not just an operational issue—they are directly affecting business revenue.

4.Some logistics partners perform much better than others
Delivery orders vary significantly by logistics partner, with Delhivery and Blue Dart handling much higher volumes than several other partners.
Their delivery quality and delay rates should be compared before deciding where to send more orders.

5.Most payment failures are not the main problem—the bigger issue is why orders are returned
Payment success is already around 91.44%, so payment processing is generally working well.
The bigger opportunity is understanding the main return reasons, where defective products and products not matching expectations are among the leading causes.
